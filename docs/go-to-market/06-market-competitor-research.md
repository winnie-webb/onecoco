**Implication:** the pilot would start in a *depressed, recovering* market. Foot traffic on Montego Bay beaches in Oct–Nov 2026 is structurally lower than a normal year; a poor pilot in November is weak evidence about a normal season. Conversely, December–April is the first real test. Also: I could not confirm whether Doctor's Cave or Walter Fletcher sustained damage or their current opening status (L) — **visit before assuming**.

### 1.2 Digital readiness (supports a phone-first product)
Internet penetration 89.5% (end-2025, 2.54M users), mobile connections 112% of population, social-media identities 63.8%; broadband penetration passed 100% in Q4 2025 (OUR) — M/H. [DataReportal Digital 2026: Jamaica](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-jamaica). Tourists bring their own data plans/roaming; hotel/beach Wi-Fi quality is unknown.

### 1.3 Payments
Lynk (NCB mobile wallet, 5,000+ merchants), WiPay (supports JMD/USD/TTD), NCB PayGate exist for local acceptance (M). PayPal receives in Jamaica; local bank withdrawal reported inconsistently (L). Card-first is right for tourists; cash/JMD still matters for locals. See doc 03 §2.

### 1.4 Labour
Minimum wage J$17,000/40 h from 1 Jul 2026 (H). Inflation 5.5% y/y May 2026 (M, Gleaner). Tourism jobs largely restored (Rio Times, M).

### 1.5 Coconut supply
Jamaica's coconut sector was devastated by lethal yellowing and Hurricane Gilbert; the Coconut Industry Board has slowed disease spread; there is a shortage of manual huskers (Gleaner 2024, M). Melissa affected >70,000 farmers and ≈41,390 ha (M) — no coconut-specific price series found. **Supply price and reliability are unknown (L)**; farmgate figure used in the model (US$0.85/nut) is an assumption. Larger, hotter risk than assumed: a coconut shortage after the hurricane would raise cost and reduce reliability.

## 4. Where to launch first (summary; scoring in doc 08)
Montego Bay (Hip Strip) because: zones/prep points are already seeded, international airport + cruise berth, density of beach-going visitors, the founders' stated focus. But note it is **the worst-hit large market** and Doctor's Cave is a private club — so verify before committing. Credible alternates to scout in parallel: **Ocho Rios/St Ann** (cruise port, beaches, comparatively less storm damage — unverified), **Negril/Westmoreland** (highest beach-vendor density, but heavily damaged by Melissa — unverified), **Falmouth/Trelawny** (new cruise port).

## 5. Barriers to adoption (hypotheses)
1. Price: US$9 delivered vs a vendor 10 m away at (probably) US$2–5.
2. "Will it really find me?" — trust + the last-20-metres problem.
3. Need to share GPS and phone number with an unknown brand on holiday.
4. Weak data signal / dead phone on the beach.
5. Existing vendors' hostility and the beach operator's concerns.
6. Limited hours (9–5) and weather pauses.

## 6. What OneCoco can credibly offer that others do not
Trackable delivery to your chair, card payment in USD with a receipt, English-language service built for visitors, personalisation (name/message) and **group ordering for parties**; for partners, a QR channel with commissions. These are *features*, not proof of preference.

## 7. Validation plan (cheap, 7 days, ≈ US$150)
1. **Price & demand walk**: ask 60 beachgoers at Doctor's Cave/Walter Fletcher/Hip Strip: "Would you pay US$9 for a coconut at your chair? What did you last pay?" Record vendor prices, queue times, density by hour (cruise vs non-cruise day).
2. **Vendor interviews** (8–10): who owns the beach pitch, what is the licensing reality, would they work with OneCoco as runner/supplier? (Turning incumbents into runners is the highest-leverage idea in this plan.)
3. **Concierge test**: 5 boutique hotels/villa hosts; will they place a QR card for a commission?
4. **Smoke test**: WhatsApp-only manual ordering with a laminated QR for a weekend; measure scans, orders, delivery times.
5. **Supply test**: get 3 written coconut quotes (per nut, delivered, grade, volume).
Decision rule: proceed if ≥ 35% of surveyed non-all-inclusive visitors say yes at ≤ US$9 *and* a beach operator will sign.
